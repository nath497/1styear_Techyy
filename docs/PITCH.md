# TutorMatch — Session 1 pitch

Three minutes, spoken. Replace **Speaker 1 / 2 / 3** with your names.
If one person presents, they read it all.

## The script

### 1. The person (~45s) — Speaker 1

This is Josh Lavado. Josh is a college student, and this school year,
Mathematics in the Modern World has been hard for him. Some lessons
just don't click. He doesn't need a whole new course. He needs someone
to explain it to him more clearly.

So what did Josh do? He searched the topic online. Then he asked a
senior he knows for help.

Did it work? In Josh's words, it "kinda worked." It took him three
hours to actually understand the lesson. And his senior? Always busy.

Josh is lucky to know a senior at all. But his help depends on
someone else's free time.

### 2. Today (~30s) — Speaker 1

Josh isn't the only one. We asked two more classmates who struggle
with Math. Jamaica studies with her notes. It works a little bit.
Angel reviews with a friend, but is still stuck. Neither of them pays
anything today. They study on their own, and they're still struggling.

But all three told us they would pay a classmate to tutor them. Josh,
up to one hundred pesos. Jamaica and Angel, up to one hundred fifty.

### 3. Our fix (~45s) — Speaker 2

TutorMatch helps learners find fellow students who can tutor them,
within their budget.

Josh opens TutorMatch. He picks Mathematics, College, and types one
hundred pesos. We call this BudgetMatch. He only sees tutors who fit,
and each one tells him why. One hundred pesos, within your budget.
Teaches Mathematics. Face-to-face. He picks a tutor and sends a session
request with his topic, the date and the time. The tutor accepts.

Josh doesn't need to already know someone. He just needs TutorMatch.

### 4. The demo moment (~30s) — Speaker 3

In Session 3 we'll show this live, on our real website. Josh's search.
A tutor who charges one hundred fifty pesos does not show up for Josh,
because that's over his budget. A request is sent, accepted, and marked
completed. Then TutorMatch records its ten percent: ten pesos for us,
ninety for the tutor. Recorded, not collected. We are not handling
real money yet.

### 5. How we'll know (~30s) — Speaker 3

Before Session 3, two or three classmates who are good at Math will
sign up as real tutors. Then we'll ask Josh, Jamaica and Angel to try
it on the live site. If they find a tutor within their budget and send
a real request, without needing to know anyone, it worked. If they
don't, we'll come back and tell you what got in the way.

*Script length: ~392 words, about three minutes at a calm pace.*

## Where your evidence stands

You have three real learners, all struggling with Math, all using a
workaround that only half works, and all saying they would pay ₱100–₱150
a session. For a first pitch, that is solid.

Its weak point: everything they told you is what they **said**, not
what they **did**. People are generous in interviews. The strongest
thing you can bring to Session 3 is one of them actually sending a
request on the live site. Before you present, make sure Jamaica and
Angel know you're quoting them, and that they're happy with it.

## The three hardest questions

### Q1 — "They *said* they'd pay. Saying isn't paying. How do you know they actually will?"

This attacks the weakest point in `docs/PROBLEM.md`, so expect it.

*Honest answer:* "We don't yet, and you're right to ask. Three
learners told us they would, and two of them pay nothing today. That's
exactly why we're asking all three to send a real request on the live
site before Session 3. If they don't, that's our answer, and we'll tell
you."

### Q2 — "Where do the tutors come from? Why would a good student tutor for ₱100 minus your 10%?"

*Honest answer:* "For the MVP we're recruiting two or three classmates
by hand. Tutors set their own rate, and ninety pesos for an hour is
real pocket money for a student. The commission is only recorded, not
collected, so nobody loses money while we learn whether tutors will
join."

### Q3 — "Why doesn't Josh just keep asking his senior? That's free."

*Honest answer:* "He does, and it kinda works. But in Josh's words, his
senior is always busy, and it took him three hours to understand one
lesson. Jamaica and Angel study on their own and are still stuck.
TutorMatch gives all three more than one person to ask, at a price
they told us they'd pay."
