# TutorMatch — Session 1 pitch

Three minutes, spoken. Replace **Speaker 1 / 2 / 3** with your names.
If one person presents, they read it all.

## The script

### 1. The person (~45s) — Speaker 1

This is Josh Lavado. Josh is a college student, and this school year,
Mathematics in the Modern World has been hard for him. Some lessons
just don't click. He doesn't need a whole new course. He needs someone
to explain it to him more clearly.

So what did Josh do? He searched the topic online. Then he asked a
senior he knows for help.

Did it work? In Josh's words, it "kinda worked." It took him three
hours to actually understand the lesson. And his senior? Always busy.

Josh is lucky to know a senior at all. But his help depends on
someone else's free time.

### 2. Today (~30s) — Speaker 1

That's how most of us get help today. We ask whoever we know. We
search online, and the explanations aren't about our exact lesson. Or
we pay a tutoring provider. One we found charges four hundred
ninety-nine pesos an hour. Josh told us he would pay for help, but his
budget is one hundred pesos a session. So today, getting help depends
on luck: who you know, and what you can afford.

### 3. Our fix (~45s) — Speaker 2

TutorMatch helps learners find fellow students who can tutor them,
within their budget.

Josh opens TutorMatch. He picks Mathematics, College, and types one
hundred pesos. We call this BudgetMatch. He only sees tutors who fit,
and each one tells him why. One hundred pesos, within your budget.
Teaches Mathematics. Face-to-face. He picks a tutor and sends a session
request with his topic, the date and the time. The tutor accepts.

Josh doesn't need to already know someone. He just needs TutorMatch.

### 4. The demo moment (~30s) — Speaker 3

In Session 3 we'll show this live, on our real website. Josh's search.
A tutor who charges one hundred fifty pesos does not show up, because
that's over his budget. A request is sent, accepted, and marked
completed. Then TutorMatch records its ten percent: ten pesos for us,
ninety for the tutor. Recorded, not collected. We are not handling
real money yet.

### 5. How we'll know (~30s) — Speaker 3

Before Session 3, two or three classmates who are good at a subject
will sign up as real tutors. Then we'll ask Josh himself to try it on
the live site. If Josh finds a tutor within one hundred pesos and sends
a request without needing to know anyone, it worked. If he can't, we'll
come back and tell you what got in the way.

*Script length: ~375 words, under three minutes at a calm pace.*

## The three hardest questions

**Honest warning first:** Josh's story is now complete. Your other
interviews are not written down yet. Add each classmate's name and
answers to `docs/PROBLEM.md` before you present, so you can quote them.

### Q1 — "Why doesn't Josh just keep asking his senior? That's free."

*Honest answer:* "He does, and it kinda works. But in Josh's words, his
senior is always busy, and it took him three hours to understand one
lesson. TutorMatch gives him more than one person to ask, at a price
he can afford."

### Q2 — "That's one student. How do you know anyone else has this problem?"

*Honest answer:* "Right now, we don't. Josh is our first interview. By
Session 3 we'll have talked to [N] more learners, and Josh and at least
one other learner will have tried the app on the live site." Only say
this if you'll actually do it.

### Q3 — "Why would a good student tutor for ₱100 minus your 10%?"

*Honest answer:* "We don't know yet, and that's what the demo tests.
For the MVP we're recruiting two or three classmates by hand. Ninety
pesos for an hour's help is real pocket money for a student, and they
choose their own rate. The commission is only recorded, not collected,
so nobody loses money while we learn whether tutors will join."
